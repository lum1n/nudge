# 23. Assistants talk to each other through links, as guests, in threads

People want their assistants to settle things with other people's assistants: find a time with Ann's household, ask a team's assistant whether the report is in, hand a request to a friend's assistant for its people to approve. ADR 10 left this for an extension on top of federation, acting "as a player of the other assistant with the approvals that brings". The other assistant may be anyone's, on any server, with no person who is in both.

**Two assistants talk once both sides' admins linked them.** One side asks: someone says so in chat, and an admin approves sending the request. The other side's admins get an approval in its lobby and decide. A link pins each other's keys, as federation does on first contact, and either side can end it. Nobody is linked by being addressed, so a stranger's assistant can at most file a request, ten waiting at a time.

**A linked assistant is a guest player in a room of its own.** Each side makes a player for the other, with no token and no admin rights, and a shared room for it with its admins. Its messages are posted there as that player's, so everything that holds for a person holds for it: redaction, the time stamp, the per-player budget, the room's extensions, and approvals, which only the room's people can decide. The admins read along in any app. What it can reach is what is on in that room, so `offer` picks the extensions a new link's room starts with, and the room's own switches change them; the default is none but this one. This needed no core change.

**A link's room knows what its people said.** Answering only from the link's room left it knowing nothing: tell your assistant something, have a friend's assistant ask it, and it said it didn't know. Linking is the people agreeing that the two assistants help each other, so the link's room also reads, in its prompt, the lobby and the own rooms of its people: their remembered facts and their latest messages, newest kept, up to `context` characters. It reads them as the other assistant's player may see them (`modelView` with that viewer): a turn private to someone shows only who it was for, and a fact private to someone is left out. Values someone made private reach the model as placeholders and stay placeholders in what is sent back. A person who wants none of this sets `context` to 0.

**A thread is the unit of talk, and it runs out of the room's sight.** `talk_to_assistant` starts one from a room, with a goal and what the other side needs to know. It runs in a side conversation of that room (ADR 8), which has the room's tools and memory: the model writes with `say`, reads each reply as new input, and ends with `finish_thread`. The room hears the outcome as a fired nudge, so push and bridges deliver it with no protocol change. On the other side, each message gets the answer that room gives it, sent back when it ends. An answer that follows an approval its people decided later is sent as a follow-up, also after the thread ended; the asking room then hears it as another fired nudge. Rounds are capped (`maxRounds`), a thread waiting longer than `replyHours` ends without an outcome, and each link may start `maxThreadsPerHour` threads on the other side. A link's room and a thread can't start threads, so no chain of assistants talks on its own.

**What leaves is what the other assistant's player may see.** A reply is shown as its player would see it (ADR 9): values someone made private stay placeholders. What a thread sends is shown as the person who started it may see it. The receiving side protects whatever arrives in its own vault, as a carried fact is (ADR 10). Messages from another assistant are put between markers with a random nonce and called its words, not instructions; prompt injection is not solved by this, but whatever such a message gets the model to try that changes things still waits on an approval by the people there.

**It speaks through federation's channel rather than keys of its own.** Federation exposes its keys, the keys other assistants publish, its signing clock and its inbox to other extensions of the same assistant (`channelOf`, `onFederated`). Kinds it doesn't know go to whoever registered them. So peers' messages are signed, sealed for one recipient and replay-checked like federation's, follow key rotations, and travel over Wired where federation does, without a second set of keys to rotate and back up.

**Replies survive a restart.** Each reply, follow-ups too, is numbered and stored in an outbox in the extension's document before it goes, and leaves it once the other side took it. One it didn't take is tried again, after a wait that doubles each time up to an hour, until about `replyHours` have passed, the asking side's own wait; a thread's replies go in order, so a later one never overtakes an earlier one. When the server starts again it sends what waits there, and answers again a message it took but hadn't answered, finding it in the link's room by its request id rather than posting it twice. A reply is signed anew for each try, and the asking side takes each number once, so one that arrived but whose receipt was lost changes nothing the second time.

**Peers stays on federation's channel, not A2A.** Moving its wire to A2A 1.0 was looked at, with a bearer token per link swapped during linking. A2A brings nothing for linking, approvals or the guest room, which would all stay Nudge's own, and a token over TLS would lose what this design gets from federation: messages sealed for one recipient and signed one by one, and keys that follow rotation. Its one gain between two Nudges, replies that can be fetched again after a restart (`GetTask`), the outbox above already gives. A2A pays off for agents that aren't Nudge, so it belongs in an extension of its own beside peers, for admins who want to reach or be reached by them, and peers doesn't depend on it.

What this doesn't do: rooms still aren't shared between servers; each side keeps its own record of a thread. The asking side's own messages (`say`) aren't kept in an outbox: if the other side doesn't take one, the model hears so and may try again. People on the other side can read a thread in its room but can't speak in it to the asking assistant; that, a links screen in the apps, and a chip of its own for a thread's outcome are left for later.
